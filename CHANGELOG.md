# Amendment log

Article 18 governs amendment of the bylaws: they may be amended only with the
approval of both Co-Directors, and if the Co-Directors disagree the Article 9
tie-break vote applies. This file records what changed, when, and under what
authority.

**Version numbers count adopted versions, not drafts.** Earlier drafts were
informally numbered up to v5, but none was ever signed, so nothing was in force.
The first version actually adopted is v1.

This matters beyond tidiness. Article 20.2 ties every acknowledgment to a
specific document version, so when a revision is adopted and participants are
asked to re-acknowledge, this file is what answers "what am I agreeing to that I
did not agree to before?"

## How to add an entry

When a revision is adopted:

1. Bump the version in the document itself.
2. Add an entry below — what changed, why, who approved it, and the date.
3. Add a `document_versions` row in the membership system with the new label and
   effective date. Existing acknowledgments stay attached to the version that
   was actually signed; the Co-Directors then decide whether to require
   re-acknowledgment (Article 20.2 permits it but does not compel it).

Entries are newest first.

## Bylaws

### v1 — not yet adopted

The first version intended for adoption. Not yet in force: no adoption date and
no acknowledgments recorded.

Changes made while preparing it, all before adoption and therefore not
amendments under Article 18:

- **Article 20 added** — Electronic Acknowledgment and Records. Establishes that
  a checkbox or button click carries the same effect as a handwritten signature
  (20.1), that acknowledgments name a specific document version (20.2), that
  records are append-only and retained indefinitely (20.3), and that notices may
  be delivered electronically (20.4). This is what allows the Team to adopt and
  acknowledge documents in the membership system rather than on paper.
- **WildApricot references removed** throughout. It was never set up, so the
  references described a system nobody used.
